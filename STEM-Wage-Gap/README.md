## Exploration of the Gender Wage Gap in STEM Occupations
###### Note: this was a semester-long partner project I completed for a university statistics course. Only my contributions to the project are included here.

## Summary
Each group was tasked with choosing any research topic of interest to be investigated. Being an all female group within the math major, we chose to focus on the gender disparity within the Science, Technology, Engineering, and Math (STEM) fields. My part in this project focused on the gender wage gap within STEM occupations. The goal of this project was to analyze real-world data in order to fill a gap in this topic's research. 

## Objectives
* Research the gender wage gap in STEM occupations
* Collect data accordingly
* Clean data into a useable dataset
* Conduct an exploratory data analysis
* Create a linear mixed-effects model
* Create data visualization to illustrate findings

## Datasets
Women's earnings data is from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. I used data from 2015-2023. In particular, Table 2 was used for its earnings data for every individual occupation. Download the raw data here: [Download Raw Earnings Data](earnings-data.xlsx)

## Conclusions
Due to the statistically insignificant results of the Linear Mixed-Effects Model, there is not enough evidence to claim there has been a decrease in the wage gap for STEM occupations from 2015 to 2023. Additionally, there is a clear trend of men dominating the higher paying occupations, which are associated with the largest wage gaps. On the other hand, there are more women in the lower paying occupations, which still have wage gaps, but they are smaller. This indicates a disparity in the number of women present in high earning jobs, as well as a general wage disparity regardless of number of workers or specific occupation.
