###### Note: this was a semester-long partner project I completed for a university statistics course. Only my contributions to the project are included here.

## Summary
Each group was tasked with examining the Covid-19 story of a partciular state within the U.S. My partner and I chose to study Vermont during the Covid-19 pandemic. We were asked to look at the data on a county level in order to make comparisons across the state. We collected data on Covid statistics, public health policies, and Vermont-specific information. The goal of this project was to tell a story using real-world data. 

## Objectives
* Research Vermont and what makes it unique
* Collect data accordingly
* Clean data into a useable dataset
* Conduct an exploratory data analysis
* Create linear regression models
* Conduct hypothesis tests
* Create data visualization to illustrate findings

## Datasets
All datasets are on the county level within the state of Vermont. Types of data include Covid cases and deaths, education levels, unemployment, income, population, demographics, vaccination levels, and mask use. Sources include New York Times, U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Vermont's Department of Health, and U.S. Department of Agriculture. 

## Challenges and Solutions
The biggest challege I faced during this project was the county-level constraint. After researching and exploring the data, I found that Vermont is a very homogenous state. For example, education, vaccination rates, mask use, and demographics were relatively constant per capita across all counties. This consistency led to many uninteresting variables because there was not much of a comparison to make between counties.
